# Lesson 0.1: How the cube is built

Teach this before any method. Use the widget: let the learner drag layers and watch which pieces move.

## Key ideas

1. **Centres never move relative to each other.** Turning a face only spins its centre in place. So the centre decides the colour of its face: the face with the white centre is the white face, always. Colour scheme in the widget: white opposite yellow, green opposite blue, red opposite orange; with white on top and green in front, red is on the right.
2. **There are three kinds of pieces.**
   - 6 centres: 1 colour each, fixed.
   - 12 edges: 2 colours each, between two centres.
   - 8 corners: 3 colours each, where three faces meet.
3. **Every piece has exactly one home.** The white-red edge belongs between the white and red centres; the white-red-green corner belongs where those three centres meet. A piece is solved when it is in its home *and* turned the right way (each sticker matches the centre next to it).
4. **Edges stay edges, corners stay corners.** A turn never changes a piece's kind, so an edge is never found in a corner spot.
5. **Solve pieces, not colours.** A face that is all one colour is not "done" unless the side stickers of that layer also match their centres. Beginners who solve one face first often have to undo it.

## Check understanding

Ask the learner, with the widget open (Reset first):

- "How many colours does the piece at the top-front-right have? What kind of piece is it?" (three; a corner)
- "Turn the right layer once. Did any centre change colour?" (no)
- "Where does the white-green edge belong?" (between the white and green centres)

## Common mistakes

- Thinking a face is solved because its stickers match, while the side stickers don't.
- Trying to move a centre to another face. Centres can only be moved together with the whole cube (x, y, z) or a middle slice (M, E, S), which changes which colour is on top, not the relationship between centres.
